Opening Scene｜That Line of Small Text in the Footer

Turn to the very bottom of this report, and the footer has a line of very small text: "Information architecture and web design by Jiang Yu-de." Throughout the report, the Members' stories, the figures of the service projects, and the impact maps all come first; he put his own name on the last line.

This placement happens to explain how he works. He rarely stands in front of an audience to talk about what he has done. What he cares about is whether other people's stories have been organized well, and once organized, whether they can be found, understood, and passed on.

Behind that line

A report is finished once it is printed; once an information architecture is built, the Members' stories can keep growing year after year.

Professional Background｜What Twenty Years Behind the Camera Showed Him

Before becoming "Coach Jiang," he held a camera for twenty years. He filmed event records for charitable organizations, cultural and educational associations, and local organizations, made documentaries, and filmed many scenes that never made it into the news.

The camera showed him two paths along which knowledge flows. Externally: how an organization conveys its values, and how it advocates and raises awareness. Internally: how an organization leads volunteers, trains partners, and hands its ideals to the next person. Many of the most precious things stay in the heads of senior people and have never been written down.

The tools keep changing, from video cameras and editing software to today's AI. What he does has never changed: seeing, organizing, reproducing.

Main Line of Impact｜AI Tools Come and Go, the Knowledge Base Stays

His main work now is helping professional knowledge workers, as well as small companies and teams of 5 to 10 people, use AI to train their own "AI employees." He often says: "Stop chasing tools to learn. Get your data ready and let AI learn you."

His approach is plain: if you can write documents and sort folders, you can start building your own knowledge base. Start by nurturing one thing you do every day, let AI get to know how you work, and then hand over the tasks a computer can do, one by one. When it comes to three kinds of things, sending anything out, money, and dates, the AI stops and hands it back to a person to confirm.

Three Paths of Support

  • AI courses and workshops: in-house training for small and medium enterprises, government projects, and public-sector training. The basic course can be taken on a phone.
  • LINE group message organization: in Taiwan, almost all work messages are in LINE. Organize the expense claims, to-dos, and project progress in the groups first, and knowledge then has a place to grow.
  • Corporate coaching: four sessions in one month, taking the team from taking stock and setting up an AI office all the way to the first AI employee starting work. One week after handover, the team reruns it on its own, and it counts as done only when it runs.
His core belief

AI can help you only when it understands you. And that requires you to understand yourself first.

Method｜Step Back and Ask "Why"

He names his work "tacit knowledge distillation." The key to the method lies in telling two kinds of questions apart: "digging in" keeps asking for details, and then what, what else; "stepping out" takes a step back and asks why it is done this way and what this reflects.

With each step back, the judgment hidden in the experience of the previous level surfaces and becomes something that can be written down, handed to AI, and handed to a newcomer. He has organized this approach into six levels: results, standards, positioning, methods, principles, and awareness.

For people, it is a newcomer handbook; for AI, it is a skill package. The same organized material works for both.

ESG Is Not a Label｜Catching the Problem of "When People Leave, the Experience Is Gone"

S｜Social (Making Knowledge Accessible and Learnable)

  • Holds two free online talks every month, so people who do not have a budget yet can still get started.
  • His teaching has included youth entrepreneurship courses of the Labor and Youth Development Department of Chiayi County, the Taichung City Daughters' House, the New Taipei City Public Art Promotion Program, Wenzao Ursuline University of Languages, the Community Computer Promotion Education Foundation, and the Rotary Club of Taipei Sustainable Impact.
  • The basic course is designed to be taken on a phone, lowering the barrier of the digital divide.

G｜Governance (Letting an Organization's Experience Be Passed On)

  • Helps teams first have a key manager get one workflow right and then replicate it, so organizational knowledge does not break off when staff change.
  • All deliverables stay on the team's own computers and accounts, so data sovereignty remains with the organization.

E｜Environment (Less Wasted Work Is Also a Form of Reduction)

  • His principle is "delete first, then automate": simplify the workflow first, then hand it to AI, so that busywork does not get faster because of AI.

His Place in the Club｜Building a Library for the Members' Stories

In the Club, he is responsible for the information architecture and web design of this Sustainable Impact Report. How each Member's profile, the service projects, and the impact figures are categorized, how they link to one another, and how both people and AI can read them, he arranged piece by piece behind the scenes.

At the 38th online regular meeting on April 27, 2026, he presented "How to Use AI to Help Write an Impact Report," laying out the approach behind this report for the Members, so that every club has the chance to use the same method to organize its own impact.

His starting point for this is the same as for his profession: the Members have quietly done a great deal in their own fields, and this experience deserves to be seen, and deserves to be left for the next term and the next new Member.

His presence in the Club

Organizing each Member's vocational service into knowledge assets that can be searched and passed on.

Closing｜Intangible Assets Grow Richer the More They Are Shared

Back to that line of small text in the footer. He put his name last because he believes that well-organized knowledge speaks for itself.

His greatest dream is to accumulate knowledge assets. He believes intangible assets grow richer the more they are shared; once a piece of experience is organized, it can keep growing in more people's hands.
