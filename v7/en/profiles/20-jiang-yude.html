1. Passing on knowledge is the foundation of a lasting organization

Jiang Yu-de (Coach Jiang) is the founder of Coach Jiang AI Knowledge Management. Before becoming "Coach Jiang," he spent twenty years behind the camera, filming the activities of public-benefit organizations, cultural and educational associations, and local groups, and making documentaries, recording many scenes that never made the news.

Those twenty years taught him that whether an organization lasts depends on whether its experience can be passed on. How it leads volunteers, trains partners, and hands its ideals to the next person often stays only in the heads of senior people and is never written down; when they leave, that experience leaves with them. Everything he has worked on since responds to this problem.

2. S｜Social: free talks that promote AI education for more people

He believes good knowledge should be usable by more people. He holds two free online talks every month to promote and broaden AI education, so that people without a budget can still get started. One talk in November 2026, "AI security for people who don't code", is for people who handle client or case data at work and already use AI, such as administrators, social workers, small business owners and event organizers; it brings security news back to everyday decisions: whether a piece of data can be given to AI, how to set form and spreadsheet permissions, and what to do with keys or passwords already pasted into AI. His introductory courses can be taken on a mobile phone, lowering the barrier of the digital divide.

He has taught for the Chiayi County Labor and Youth Development Department's youth entrepreneurship program, the Taichung Women's Center, the New Taipei City Public Art Promotion Program, Wenzao Ursuline University of Languages, the Community Computer Education Foundation, and the Club. From youth entrepreneurship and women's empowerment to community education, these settings echo Quality Education (SDG 4) and Decent Work (SDG 8).

3. S｜Social: a knowledge base for social issues

He is also promoting a knowledge base for social issues: organizing knowledge about social issues so that advocates can use it directly. He hopes it will let people working on social issues spend less effort searching for information and more on advocacy itself.

4. G｜Governance: keeping organizational experience from breaking when people change

He helps professionals and small teams of 5 to 10 people turn what they do every day into their own knowledge base. He starts by having a key manager get one workflow right, then replicates it, so that organizational knowledge does not break when people change roles. Everything stays on the team's own computers and accounts, so the organization keeps ownership of its data.

He calls this "tacit knowledge distillation": stepping back to ask "why" until the judgment hidden in experience becomes a handbook a newcomer can understand. When experience is kept, every handover no longer has to start from zero.

5. E｜Environment: less wasted work is also a reduction

When helping teams adopt new tools, he insists on "cut first, then automate": simplify the workflow first, so that busywork does not simply get faster.

This principle shares the spirit of sustainability: put resources where they are truly needed, reduce repetition and waste, and give people's time back to meaningful work.

The social issue knowledge base he is promoting follows the same thinking: once issue knowledge is organized into a shared knowledge base, each advocate no longer needs to have AI gather and organize it from scratch again, which also reduces AI computing and token consumption.

6. In the Club: serving society through his profession by recording Members' impact

In the Club, he is responsible for the information architecture and web design of this Sustainability Impact Report: how each Member's profile, service projects, and impact figures are classified, linked, and made easy to read and find. At the 38th online Club meeting on April 27, 2026, he shared "How to Use AI to Help Write an Impact Report," so that every club can organize its own impact the same way.

The Club believes in serving society through one's profession. He answers Rotary's spirit of vocational service with his own profession: the service Members quietly carry out in their fields deserves to be seen, learned from, and continued, for the next Club year and the next new Member.

7. In Their Words

"Intangible assets grow richer the more they are shared."

His biggest dream is to accumulate knowledge assets. Once experience is organized, it can keep growing in more people's hands; once a knowledge architecture is in place, Members' impact can keep accumulating year after year.
