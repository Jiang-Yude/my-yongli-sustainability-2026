1. An aroma educator who answers society through her profession

Dr. Ivy Huang (Huang Min-jing) is the founder of WELLINA and Chief Integration Consultant for International Aromatherapy and Brand Scent. She has shared in interviews that her experience after the 921 Earthquake became the starting point for rethinking scent and companionship: in the most chaotic days, a familiar smell can be the small thing that helps a person feel steady.

Since then she has kept learning, teaching, and practicing, with 28 years of professional experience and about 3,000 talks and classes, turning complex aroma knowledge into professional content that can be understood, applied, and passed on. Her teaching philosophy: "Make complex knowledge clear, and keep learning human."

2. S｜Social: bringing social welfare partners into the herb industry chain

In recent years she has devoted herself most to a sustainable herb industry chain built with Sifan Natural Farm. The farm connects NPOs across Taiwan and 14 social welfare organizations; depending on each site's conditions, participants join suitable stages such as raising seedlings, planting, tending, harvesting, and pre-processing. The publicly reported scale of participation is 179 people.

According to Sifan's published farm records, farm work has totaled 344 person-visits and 2,500 hours, of which partners receiving social welfare services at Meidi Farm contributed 42 person-visits and 221 hours. The nature of participation and hours are recorded separately, so every contribution is visible.

3. S｜Social: bringing aroma education into families, social welfare, and psychological care

She has taught in hospitals, universities, companies, and public institutions, and has long worked with social welfare and family settings: the Tainan, South Kaohsiung, and Chiayi City Family Support Centers; the Chiayi City, Chiayi County, and Tainan City Family Education Centers; the National Immigration Agency's talks for cross-border families; and the Tainan City Labor Affairs Bureau's workplace stress relief talks. With clinical psychologists and counselors on her cross-disciplinary team, she has set up scent exploration locations with 9 psychological care institutions from Taipei to Pingtung.

For young people and across generations, she took part in National Sun Yat-sen University's ALOHA Sustainable Development Winter Camp and seed teacher training and the Chiayi City Youth Festival scent-blending course, and mentored a National Chiayi University student aroma startup team that won a silver award for food innovation from the Ministry of Education. In elder care she has worked with National Chiayi University, National Tainan Junior College of Nursing, and Shu-Jen Home Economics and Commercial High School. In 2024 she received an Outstanding Individual award for family education on Grandparents' Day from Chiayi City.

4. E｜Environment: from a single herb to a complete sustainability path

The path begins with the land. Sifan's website reports a current total cultivated area of 3,100 ping. After social welfare partners plant and harvest the herbs, Dr. Ivy coordinates professional units for extraction and composition and specification analysis, then designs products based on the material and its uses, so that herbs, once grown, can become products people actually use.

Companies can join through keynote talks, employee scent-blending, volunteering, gift purchasing, or product co-creation, turning one-off support into ongoing partnership so the chain can keep running.

5. G｜Governance: every figure must have a known source

She has set clear disclosure principles for the chain: Sifan's public figures are labeled with the original URL and update date; NPO lists, nature of participation, planted area, yield, hours, purchasing, and company participation are added only after the responsible unit confirms them; extraction rates, composition, and safety data follow the professional unit's batch documents.

She does not estimate essential oil yield from planting volume, does not convert activity records directly into ESG, GRI, SDG, or certification outcomes, and records vision figures separately from completed results. Before testing, sampling, and compliance checks are complete, no launch or effect is promised. Transparency is the basis of trust that keeps social welfare groups, the farm, and companies walking together over time.

6. In the Club: bringing vocational service into the community

At the 32nd online Club meeting on January 19, 2026, she shared "A Woman's Inner Presence: Building Confidence and Workplace Charm with Essential Oils and Fragrance," bringing aroma education into the Club's meeting.

The Club believes in serving society through one's profession. Dr. Ivy's herb industry chain connects Rotary's vocational service, community service, and social welfare participation in one line: her profession becomes work that social welfare partners take part in, and an entry point for companies to take part in sustainability.
